# Pre-registration: rig/h1, a human in the loop

Written before any h1 session.

## Question

Every earlier rig used a machine as the trainer, and found nothing at about ±0.1%. Suppose
a human has to be in the loop. When a person solves a puzzle and then holds its answer in
mind, do blind machines searching the same space at random land on that answer more often
than on matched answers nobody worked on?

## Where the design comes from

This adapts the published tests of morphic resonance and of intention acting on random
sources, and fixes the weakness each one had:

| Earlier test | Design | Weakness, and the fix here |
|---|---|---|
| Schwartz, Hebrew words; Mahlberg, Morse code; Robbins & Roe, Chinese characters | Real items (learned by millions) against matched fakes, with naive people as testers | **Weakness:** "matched" fakes differ in structure, so the controls are never truly equivalent. The Chinese-character effect failed two replications (Roe & Hitchman 2011, Vernon et al.). **Fix:** every candidate is exactly equivalent for the machine. Each is one index out of 455, drawn uniformly, so with no anomaly every candidate has the same hit rate. |
| Sheldrake's hidden images (1983) | Test recognition before and after a broadcast | **Weakness:** the tested groups differ between before and after. **Fix:** the same drawers count trained and untrained candidates side by side, in the same seconds. |
| Black, Butzer & Sheldrake (2026), Wordle | Do later solvers do better after many earlier solves? | **Weakness:** no randomized treatment, and the positive study did not replicate. **Fix:** which puzzles the human works on is randomized and sealed. |
| PEAR random-event-generator studies, the Mind-Machine Interaction consortium, Maier et al. (2018) | Intention aimed at a random source | **Weakness:** the claimed effects are a few parts in 10⁴. The three-lab consortium replication failed, and 12,571 online participants gave BF₀₁ = 10. **Fix:** the receiver here sees about 0.05% (RDSEED) and about 0.01% (urandom) per session, and everything is pre-registered. |

## Protocol

1. **Open the session.** `rig/h1/registrar.py new` draws a pool of 12 puzzles: three
   different numbers from 1 to 15, identified by their sum and product, with no triple reused
   across sessions. From OS entropy it also draws which 6 the human works on, and in what
   order. It publishes the pool and `sha256(sealed assignment ‖ nonce)` before the session.
2. **Run the drawers.** On "go", `rig/h1/run-session.sh` starts two blind drawers for 30
   minutes, running as the system user `rig-tester`. Each drawer gets only the 12 candidates
   and counts, in 5-second chunks, the uniform draws in [0, 455) that land on each one. The
   sources are:
   - `rdseed`: the CPU's hardware noise source;
   - `urandom`: the kernel CSPRNG. Its output is a ChaCha20 stream rekeyed from the entropy
     pool every 60 s, so it is a weaker antenna, and it is included as the comparison.

   Nothing is mixed into either source: no beacon, no XOR.
3. **The human works.** Within the first 20 minutes the human opens the h1 page, which holds
   only the 6 trained puzzles. For each one in order they solve it, then hold its three
   numbers in mind for 60 seconds. The page logs, per puzzle: when it was shown, when it was
   solved, the number of attempts, and when the hold started and ended.
4. **Reveal and score.** `registrar.py reveal`, then `rig/h1/analyze.py <sid>`.

## Analysis

The analysis is `rig/h1/analyze.py`; its hash is part of each session's `protocol_sha256`.

- **Primary, per session.** For each source, take
  D = mean log(hits) over trained candidates − mean log(hits) over untrained ones, over the
  whole 30 minutes. It is tested one-sided against all 924 possible choices of the trained
  six, which is exact. Holm across the two sources, α = 0.05.
- **Secondary.** These are reported, with no claim attached:
  - **Hold windows.** Each trained answer's hits while it was being held, relative to the
    untrained answers in the same seconds. The null comes from shuffled labels.
  - **After.** D using only the draws after the human finished.
- **Series.** Stouffer over sessions, using z from the mid-p, per source, with Holm across
  sources. The series is scored after every session. It is read as a result only from the
  fifth session on; before that it is descriptive.

## Sensitivity

Measured on this host in a 20-second smoke run with the CPU busy, the rates were 1.7 × 10⁶
draws/s (rdseed) and 6 × 10⁷/s (urandom). Over 30 minutes that is an 80%-power MDE of about
0.05% (rdseed) and about 0.01% (urandom) relative lift in hit rate. On an idle host it should
be lower. For scale: a target-directed bias of 2 × 10⁻⁴ per bit, the size
PEAR claimed, would lift a 16-bit draw's hit rate by about 0.6%.

## Predictions

- **Mine.** No session detects anything, and the series stays null.
- **The alternative.** Human attention on a specific answer makes machines' random search
  land on that answer more often, during the hold, after it, or both.

## What a result would mean

| Outcome | Reading |
|---|---|
| A session is detected | One anomaly. It needs the series, and then other people and separate machines, before it counts. |
| Detected on rdseed but not urandom | Consistent with an effect on physical noise that the CSPRNG's expansion dilutes. |
| Hold windows show it but the whole window does not | A transient effect tied to attention. Lengthen the holds. |
| Null series over 5 or more sessions | No effect of about 0.025% or more (rdseed), or about 0.005% (urandom), from one person's focused attention on this setup. |
